# ADR-013 — Ecosystem outcome, consumer-aware owned reuse

**Status:** Proposed mechanics implementing explicit INT-003 direction; no repository migration or engine approval claimed.  
**Scope:** Extends ADR-004 and SPEC-06 without replacing existing source/work authorities.

## Context

External reuse alone leaves an internal duplication loophole. Repository-local success can export maintenance, incompatibility and runtime cost to other products. Conversely, overly broad shared frameworks can increase coupling and delay usable products.

## Decision proposal

Treat owned and external capabilities as reuse candidates. Prefer use/modify/adapt/narrowly-wrap over parallel handrolling where evidence supports it. Require a proportional consumer-impact record for substantive changes, covering actual and committed consumers, plausible future uses, preserved contracts, total effects and source/consumer owners. Keep future seams cheap and independent products independently useful. Continue this assessment during normal development rather than as a one-time migration phase.

## Alternatives

Local-repo optimization is simpler but can duplicate mechanisms and externalize costs. Universal mandatory abstraction maximizes apparent sharing but may destroy product fit and isolate nothing. An approval gate on every textual edit imposes needless overhead. The selected approach scopes evidence and coordination to material capability/contract impact.

## Consequences

A local implementation may remain when its distinct semantics or constraints justify separation. An external dependency can replace owned code. A shared repair can require coordinated consumer work and a compatibility transition. Speculative consumers create hypotheses or low-cost seams, not fabricated commitments. Existing permissions and path leases remain binding.

## Verification and reconsideration

Trace REQ-06-09..REQ-06-20 to actual provider/consumer tests, adoption and measured or qualified maintenance effects. Schema validity does not prove correct architecture. Reconsider a boundary when consumers, upstream, constraints or measured coupling change. Do not spawn a new registry or fleet-wide rewrite to enact this decision.
